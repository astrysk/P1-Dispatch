DISPATCH
PRODUCT STRATEGY & DEFINITION
October 2026

2.1 PRODUCT BRIEF

Product Overview

Dispatch is a pickup-reliability solution for manufacturers that depend on external transporters to move finished goods from their factories to customers, distributors or other business locations.

We are building Dispatch to address the operational uncertainty between arranging a vehicle and ensuring that the vehicle actually arrives for collection.

Rather than becoming another freight marketplace, we will focus on pickup commitments, risk detection, coordination and exception recovery.

Problem Statement

Small and medium-sized manufacturers may experience missed or delayed factory pickups when transporters fail to arrive as scheduled, cancel at short notice or communicate changes too late.

When this happens, dispatch teams must manually contact transporters, negotiate alternatives and manage the resulting delays.

Existing transport arrangements may provide access to vehicles without giving the manufacturer a consistent process for monitoring pickup commitments and recovering when they fail.

Our core problem: Manufacturers lack a dependable, accountable process for managing scheduled pickups and responding when transportation arrangements break down.

The frequency and financial impact of this problem remain unvalidated.

Target Users

Primary user: Factory dispatch manager
Coordinates daily outbound shipments, confirms vehicle arrivals, follows up with transporters and resolves pickup delays.

Economic buyer: Factory owner or operations head
Bears the operational and commercial consequences of delayed dispatches and evaluates whether a reliability service is worth paying for.

Service partner: Transporter or fleet operator
Accepts pickup commitments, communicates availability and may provide replacement capacity when the original arrangement fails.

Core Use Case

A manufacturer schedules a pickup for a specific date and time.

Dispatch records the commitment, confirms the arrangement, monitors its status and identifies potential delays.

If the transporter cannot fulfil the commitment, Dispatch coordinates an alternative where available and keeps the manufacturer informed.

The pickup is then recorded as completed, delayed, cancelled or unresolved.

Value Delivered

We aim to help manufacturers:
- Reduce missed and delayed pickups.
- Spend less time coordinating transporters manually.
- Identify pickup problems earlier.
- Recover faster when transportation arrangements fail.
- Build a reliable record of transporter performance.

Product Objective

Make scheduled factory pickups more predictable and operationally manageable.


2.2 PRODUCT VISION & STRATEGY

Product Vision

We want Dispatch to become a trusted reliability layer between manufacturers and the transport capacity they depend on.

Our long-term direction is to help businesses manage transportation commitments, identify operational risks and recover from failures through a consistent, measurable workflow.

Strategic Principles

1. Reliability Before Marketplace Scale

We will solve the pickup execution problem before attempting to aggregate large numbers of transporters or shipments.

A marketplace that generates bookings but cannot improve pickup outcomes would not deliver our intended value.

2. Coordinate Existing Capacity

We will initially work with existing transporters and fleet operators rather than investing in an owned fleet.

This keeps the initial operating model focused on coordination, service quality and recovery.

3. Manage Exceptions, Not Just Bookings

We will distinguish Dispatch from a basic booking interface by focusing on what happens after a pickup is scheduled.

Confirmation, monitoring, communication and recovery are central to the product.

4. Start With One Concentrated Market

We will begin with a narrowly defined industrial cluster and manufacturers with recurring outbound shipments.

Concentration should make it easier to build transporter relationships, understand local operating patterns and access replacement capacity.

The specific cluster will be selected during implementation planning using customer density, shipment frequency, transport availability and operational feasibility.

5. Prove Value Before Automating Everything

We will initially support the workflow with lightweight software and managed coordination.

We will automate activities when recurring operational evidence demonstrates that automation improves reliability or reduces service costs.

Differentiation

Dispatch will compete on the reliability of the pickup process rather than the number of transporters listed on a platform.

Our intended differentiation is:
- Recorded pickup commitments.
- Proactive identification of at-risk pickups.
- Structured exception handling.
- Coordinated recovery when alternatives are available.
- Measurable pickup and transporter performance.

These capabilities represent our intended positioning, not an established competitive advantage. We must demonstrate that they improve real pickup outcomes.

Strategic Boundaries

At the initial stage, we will not:
- Build a general-purpose freight marketplace.
- Purchase or operate our own vehicle fleet.
- Expand across multiple industrial clusters simultaneously.
- Promise guaranteed pickups without the operational capacity to support that promise.
- Build complex predictive systems before we have sufficient reliable operational data.


2.3 VALUE PROPOSITION

Core Value Proposition

Dispatch helps manufacturers make scheduled pickups more reliable by confirming commitments, monitoring execution and coordinating recovery when transportation arrangements fail.

Value by User

For Factory Owners

Value: Greater control over outbound logistics.

Dispatch aims to reduce the disruption caused by failed pickups and improve visibility into recurring transportation problems.

For Dispatch Managers

Value: Less manual coordination and a clearer process for resolving exceptions.

Instead of repeatedly chasing updates across calls and messages, dispatch managers can work through a single pickup workflow.

For Transporters

Value: Clearer pickup requirements and more structured communication.

Transporters can receive defined commitments, communicate delays and maintain a record of their service performance.

Why Choose Dispatch?

A manufacturer may already have trusted transporters, brokers or existing logistics platforms.

We should not require customers to replace those relationships simply to use Dispatch.

Our initial proposition is to improve the reliability of the arrangements they already use.

A customer should choose Dispatch if the operational value of fewer failures, faster recovery and reduced coordination effort exceeds the service fee.

Value Proposition Hypothesis

We expect customers to be most interested when:
- Pickup failures occur frequently enough to create material disruption.
- Dispatch teams spend significant time following up with transporters.
- Missed pickups have measurable commercial or operational consequences.
- Suitable alternative transport capacity can be arranged.
- Customers are willing to pay for better execution rather than just access to vehicles.

We will treat willingness to pay as an open question until supported by actual customer and transaction evidence.


2.4 PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT (PRD)

2.4.1 Product Objective

Build the minimum operational system required to create, confirm, monitor and resolve scheduled factory pickups.

The first version must support the complete pickup lifecycle, including failed or delayed pickups.

2.4.2 Primary User Journey

1. A manufacturer creates a pickup request.
2. The pickup details and expected collection time are recorded.
3. A transporter is assigned or associated with the request.
4. The pickup commitment is confirmed.
5. Dispatch tracks the commitment against its scheduled time.
6. A pickup is marked completed, delayed, cancelled or at risk.
7. If a problem occurs, the responsible operator coordinates recovery.
8. The final outcome and relevant timestamps are recorded.

The first version may use manual status updates and human-led coordination. Automated tracking will depend on available integrations and operational feasibility.

2.4.3 Functional Requirements

FR-01: Pickup Creation

The system must allow an authorised user to create a pickup request.

Required information:
- Pickup reference.
- Manufacturer or customer.
- Factory pickup location.
- Destination.
- Scheduled pickup date and time.
- Shipment description and relevant handling information.
- Assigned transporter, when available.
- Contact details required for coordination.

Acceptance criteria:
- A valid request can be created and assigned a unique reference.
- Required fields are validated before submission.
- Incomplete requests cannot enter the confirmed state.

FR-02: Commitment Confirmation

The system must distinguish between a requested pickup and a confirmed pickup.

Acceptance criteria:
- A pickup cannot be labelled confirmed without an explicit confirmation from the transporter or an authorised operator recording that confirmation.
- The system records the confirmation time and the person or source confirming it.
- Unconfirmed requests remain visibly distinguishable from confirmed commitments.

FR-03: Pickup Status Management

The system must maintain a defined status for each pickup.

Initial statuses:
- Requested.
- Awaiting confirmation.
- Confirmed.
- At risk.
- Recovery in progress.
- Completed.
- Delayed.
- Cancelled.
- Unresolved.

Acceptance criteria:
- Every pickup has one current status.
- Only permitted status transitions are allowed.
- Status changes record the time and source of the change.
- Completed pickups cannot silently return to an active state.
- Corrections must preserve an auditable history.

FR-04: Pickup Monitoring

The system must identify pickups that require attention as their scheduled collection time approaches.

Acceptance criteria:
- Confirmed pickups approaching their scheduled time can be identified.
- Overdue pickups without a recorded arrival or completion are flagged.
- An operator can review the reason for a flag and update the pickup status.
- The initial version may rely on scheduled checks and manual updates rather than live GPS tracking.

FR-05: Exception Management

The system must allow an operator to record and manage pickup failures.

Supported exception types:
- Transporter no-show.
- Late arrival.
- Last-minute cancellation.
- Vehicle unavailable.
- Pickup details incorrect.
- Customer or factory not ready.
- Other operational issue.

Acceptance criteria:
- An exception can be associated with a pickup.
- The system records the issue, time, responsible party where known and current resolution status.
- An operator can record corrective actions and communication.
- The exception remains available for later review.

FR-06: Recovery Coordination

The system must support attempts to arrange alternative transportation when the original commitment fails.

Acceptance criteria:
- An operator can record a recovery attempt.
- Alternative transporters and their responses can be recorded.
- A replacement is not treated as confirmed until acceptance is explicitly recorded.
- The original failure and subsequent recovery outcome remain linked.
- If no alternative is available, the pickup is marked unresolved rather than incorrectly marked recovered.

FR-07: Notifications and Communication

The system must support communication when a pickup is confirmed, delayed, at risk or resolved.

Acceptance criteria:
- The system can identify the relevant parties for each event.
- Messages or notifications are generated only for configured events and available channels.
- Failed notification attempts are recorded where technically supported.
- Critical status changes remain visible in the system even if a notification fails.
- The first version may use manually initiated calls or messages, with automation introduced where appropriate.

FR-08: Pickup History

The system must maintain a history of pickup events.

Acceptance criteria:
- Users can review the pickup's status history.
- The history includes relevant timestamps, exceptions, recovery attempts and final outcomes.
- Changes can be attributed to a user or system event where available.
- Historical records are not silently overwritten.

FR-09: Operational Dashboard

The system must provide a consolidated view of pickups requiring attention.

The initial dashboard should prioritise:
- Pickups awaiting confirmation.
- Confirmed pickups approaching their scheduled time.
- At-risk or overdue pickups.
- Active recovery attempts.
- Completed and unresolved pickups.

Acceptance criteria:
- Users can identify which pickups require action.
- The displayed status reflects the latest recorded event.
- Users can open a pickup and access its operational history.

FR-10: Performance Reporting

The system must calculate basic pickup reliability metrics from recorded events.

Initial reporting includes:
- Total scheduled pickups.
- Confirmed pickups.
- Completed pickups.
- On-time pickup rate.
- Missed pickup rate.
- Recovery rate.
- Average delay.
- Exception frequency by transporter.

Acceptance criteria:
- Each metric has a documented definition.
- Metrics use consistent date ranges and denominators.
- Missing timestamps are not silently treated as successful outcomes.
- Reports distinguish measured results from incomplete data.

2.4.4 Non-Functional Requirements

Reliability

Pickup records and status changes must be persisted consistently. The system must avoid duplicate records caused by repeated submissions where technically feasible.

Security

Users must access only the information permitted by their roles and organisational relationships. Sensitive contact and shipment information must not be exposed publicly.

Auditability

Important status changes and recovery actions must have a traceable history.

Usability

A dispatch manager should be able to create, locate and update a pickup without navigating unnecessary screens. The interface should prioritise operational clarity over decorative features.

Performance

The dashboard and pickup details should load quickly under the expected initial workload. Specific performance thresholds will be defined alongside the chosen technical architecture and deployment environment.

Extensibility

The data model should support future integrations for messaging, location tracking, transporter systems and analytics without requiring a fundamental redesign.

2.4.5 Dependencies and Constraints

The product depends on:
- Accurate pickup details from manufacturers.
- Transporters who confirm or update their commitments.
- Access to suitable alternative transport capacity.
- Timely recording of pickup arrivals and completion.
- Clear operational ownership when an exception occurs.
- Reliable communication between the parties involved.

The initial product will not be able to guarantee physical arrival based on software alone. Reliability depends on both the system and the underlying transportation operation.

2.4.6 Product Acceptance Standard

The initial product is functionally acceptable when an authorised operator can manage a pickup from creation through confirmation, monitoring, exception handling and final resolution, with a traceable record of each significant event.

This establishes workflow completeness. It does not, by itself, prove commercial viability or improved pickup reliability.


2.5 MVP SCOPE & FEATURE PRIORITISATION

MVP Objective

Prove that a structured pickup-reliability workflow can improve operational execution for a narrowly defined group of manufacturers.

We will prioritise capabilities that directly support this objective.

P0: Required for the MVP

- Pickup management: Create and manage scheduled pickup requests.
- Commitment confirmation: Record whether a transporter has accepted a pickup.
- Status tracking: Maintain the current status and history of each pickup.
- Exception management: Identify and record delays, cancellations and no-shows.
- Recovery coordination: Record alternative transport attempts and their outcomes.
- Operational dashboard: Show pickups requiring confirmation, intervention or resolution.
- Basic reporting: Measure pickup completion, punctuality, failures and recovery.
- User access control: Restrict operational data to authorised users.

P1: After the Core Workflow Works

- Automated reminders and confirmation messages.
- Configurable escalation rules.
- Transporter performance summaries.
- Customer-facing pickup status updates.
- Reusable transporter and factory profiles.
- Basic integrations with commonly used communication channels.

These features should be prioritised when the initial workflow demonstrates recurring operational value.

P2: Future Capabilities

- Automated transporter recommendations.
- Predictive pickup-risk scoring.
- Live GPS tracking integrations.
- Automated backup transporter matching.
- Advanced logistics analytics.
- Multi-location enterprise management.
- Deeper integration with transport management and enterprise systems.

Explicitly Out of Scope

For the initial MVP, we will not build:
- An open freight marketplace.
- An owned vehicle fleet.
- A complete transport management system.
- A full warehouse management system.
- Automated dynamic pricing.
- AI prediction systems without sufficient historical data.
- Nationwide operations from launch.

Prioritisation Rule

We will prioritise features based on their contribution to four outcomes:

1. Can we establish a real pickup commitment?
2. Can we identify when that commitment is at risk?
3. Can we coordinate a response when it fails?
4. Can we measure whether the response improves the outcome?

Features that do not materially support these outcomes will remain outside the initial MVP unless new evidence changes the product direction.


2.6 USER STORIES & ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

Epic 1: Create a Pickup

User story:
As a dispatch manager, I want to create a pickup request so that the shipment and its expected collection time are recorded in one place.

Acceptance criteria:
- A request requires a pickup location, destination and scheduled time.
- The system assigns a unique pickup reference.
- Invalid or incomplete requests cannot be submitted.
- The request appears in the operational dashboard.

Epic 2: Confirm a Commitment

User story:
As a dispatch manager, I want to know whether the transporter has accepted the pickup so that I can distinguish a real commitment from an unconfirmed arrangement.

Acceptance criteria:
- New requests are not automatically marked confirmed.
- Confirmation records its time and source.
- Unconfirmed pickups remain visible.
- A changed commitment can be recorded without losing the previous history.

Epic 3: Identify At-Risk Pickups

User story:
As a dispatch manager, I want to identify pickups that are approaching their scheduled time without sufficient confirmation or progress so that I can intervene before the delay worsens.

Acceptance criteria:
- Pickups can be filtered by scheduled time and status.
- Overdue pickups without a recorded arrival or completion are flagged.
- An operator can review and update the pickup.
- A flag does not automatically imply that the transporter has failed.

Epic 4: Resolve a Failed Pickup

User story:
As a dispatch manager, I want to record a pickup failure and coordinate an alternative so that I can minimise disruption to the shipment.

Acceptance criteria:
- The failure reason can be recorded.
- Recovery attempts can be associated with the original pickup.
- Alternative transporter acceptance is recorded explicitly.
- The system distinguishes successful recovery from an unsuccessful attempt.
- The final outcome remains linked to the original failure.

Epic 5: Communicate a Status Change

User story:
As a manufacturer, I want to receive relevant pickup updates so that I know whether the shipment is progressing as expected.

Acceptance criteria:
- Relevant parties are identifiable.
- Supported status events can trigger notifications.
- Notification failures do not erase the underlying status change.
- The system does not claim that a message was delivered when delivery has not been confirmed.

Epic 6: Review Pickup Performance

User story:
As a factory owner, I want to review pickup reliability so that I can understand how frequently failures occur and whether the service is improving performance.

Acceptance criteria:
- Reports support a defined reporting period.
- On-time, delayed, failed and recovered pickups are distinguishable.
- Metrics use documented calculation rules.
- Incomplete data is identified rather than silently counted as success.

Epic 7: Review Transporter Performance

User story:
As a dispatch manager, I want to review transporter pickup history so that I can make better-informed allocation decisions.

Acceptance criteria:
- Pickup outcomes can be grouped by transporter.
- Reports distinguish confirmed commitments from completed pickups.
- Cancellations, delays and no-shows are recorded separately.
- The system does not infer fault when the recorded evidence is insufficient.


2.7 PRODUCT ROADMAP

The roadmap is organised around evidence and operational capability rather than arbitrary feature volume.

Phase A: Strategy and Product Definition
Status: Complete

We have defined:
- The pickup-reliability problem.
- The initial customer segment.
- The product vision and differentiation.
- The MVP boundaries.
- The core operational workflow.
- Functional requirements and acceptance criteria.
- Initial success metrics.

Exit condition: The product has a documented scope and a consistent definition of what success means.

Phase B: Workflow and Technical Design

Objective: Translate the requirements into an implementable system.

We will define:
- The end-to-end pickup workflow.
- Screen structure and navigation.
- User roles and permissions.
- Pickup and exception data models.
- Status transitions and event history.
- Notification and escalation behaviour.
- Technical architecture and deployment approach.

Exit condition: The critical workflow and technical design are sufficiently specified to build and test the MVP.

Phase C: MVP Development

Objective: Implement the smallest complete pickup-management system.

We will build:
- Pickup creation and management.
- Commitment confirmation.
- Status tracking and history.
- Exception recording.
- Recovery coordination.
- Operational dashboard.
- Basic reliability reporting.
- Access control.

Exit condition: A pickup can be managed end to end, including an unsuccessful pickup and its recovery attempts.

Phase D: Operational Validation

Objective: Determine whether Dispatch works under real operating conditions.

We will evaluate:
- Whether users record commitments consistently.
- Whether at-risk pickups can be identified in time.
- Whether alternative transport can be arranged when needed.
- Whether pickup outcomes improve compared with the existing workflow.
- Whether the coordination effort is economically sustainable.

Exit condition: Operational evidence is sufficient to decide whether to continue, modify or stop the current approach.

Phase E: Product Improvement

Objective: Remove recurring operational bottlenecks.

Potential improvements include automated notifications, escalation rules, transporter performance reporting and communication integrations.

We will prioritise improvements based on observed failures and operational costs.

Exit condition: The product demonstrates repeatable value beyond a single operator or isolated customer.

Phase F: Controlled Expansion

Objective: Expand only after the operating model is repeatable.

We will evaluate additional manufacturers, transporter networks and industrial clusters when the initial market demonstrates sufficient demand, recovery capacity and sustainable economics.

Exit condition: Expansion can be supported without materially degrading pickup reliability or service economics.


2.8 SUCCESS METRICS

We will measure success at three levels: pickup reliability, operational efficiency and commercial viability.

Because no verified pilot baseline exists, the initial baseline is unknown. Targets below are provisional validation thresholds, not established forecasts or customer commitments.

1. Pickup Reliability

On-Time Pickup Rate

Definition: Percentage of eligible completed pickups collected by the agreed pickup deadline.

Formula:
On-time completed pickups / Eligible completed pickups x 100

We must define the pickup deadline and treatment of customer-caused delays before comparing results.

Initial target: Establish a reliable baseline before setting a performance improvement target.

Missed Pickup Rate

Definition: Percentage of confirmed pickups that fail to occur within the agreed operational window and are classified as missed.

Formula:
Missed confirmed pickups / Total eligible confirmed pickups x 100

We will distinguish a missed pickup from a delay and document the reasons for failure.

Initial target: Establish the baseline and determine whether Dispatch reduces the rate.

Recovery Rate

Definition: Percentage of eligible failed pickups for which an alternative arrangement results in a successful pickup within the defined recovery window.

Formula:
Successfully recovered failed pickups / Eligible failed pickups x 100

Initial target: Measure actual recovery performance before setting a numerical improvement target.

Average Pickup Delay

Definition: Average time between the agreed pickup deadline and actual pickup time for delayed pickups.

Initial target: Establish the baseline and measure whether the product reduces delays.

2. Operational Efficiency

Coordination Time per Pickup

Definition: Total staff time spent confirming, monitoring and resolving a pickup divided by the number of pickups handled.

We will compare the existing process with the Dispatch-supported process.

Initial target: Demonstrate a reduction without compromising pickup outcomes.

Time to Detect a Pickup Problem

Definition: Time between the point at which a pickup becomes detectably at risk and the time the issue is identified.

Initial target: Establish a baseline and reduce avoidable detection delays.

Time to Recovery

Definition: Time between recording a pickup failure and confirming a viable replacement arrangement, where one is available.

Initial target: Establish the baseline and evaluate whether structured coordination improves recovery speed.

Exception Resolution Rate

Definition: Percentage of recorded pickup exceptions that reach a documented resolution within the defined operational window.

Unresolved cases must remain visible and must not be counted as resolved merely because an operator closes a record.

3. Commercial Viability

Customer Willingness to Pay

Definition: Evidence that target manufacturers will pay for the reliability service.

We will distinguish verbal interest from an actual paid transaction.

Success evidence: Customers pay for the service and continue using it when the operational value is demonstrated.

Paid Pickup Volume

Definition: Number of pickups for which Dispatch earns a service fee during a defined period.

Success evidence: Repeatable paid usage rather than isolated transactions.

Contribution per Pickup

Definition: Revenue earned per pickup minus the variable costs attributable to delivering the service.

Variable costs should include relevant coordination labour, recovery costs, payment charges and other transaction-specific expenses.

Success evidence: Positive contribution per pickup under a clearly defined and repeatable operating model.

Customer Retention

Definition: Percentage of paying customers who continue using Dispatch over a defined period.

Success evidence: Repeat usage that indicates ongoing operational value rather than one-time experimentation.

4. Measurement Rules

We will follow five rules when evaluating these metrics:
- Every metric must have a documented definition and calculation method.
- Baselines must come from recorded operating data, not assumptions.
- Missing data must be identified rather than treated as a successful outcome.
- Performance must be evaluated alongside service cost and operational effort.
- We will not claim improved reliability until comparable evidence supports the claim.

Product Success Definition

Dispatch will be considered ready for controlled expansion when we have evidence that:

1. Manufacturers experience a meaningful pickup-reliability problem.
2. The product helps identify or resolve pickup failures.
3. Pickup outcomes improve against a credible baseline.
4. Customers demonstrate willingness to pay.
5. The service can be delivered with sustainable contribution economics.
6. The operating model can be repeated without excessive manual effort.

A functioning application alone is not product success. We must demonstrate that Dispatch improves the underlying logistics operation.


PHASE 2: FINAL PRODUCT DECISION

Recommendation: Proceed with a narrowly scoped pickup-reliability MVP.

We will build around the complete pickup lifecycle: creation, confirmation, monitoring, exception handling, recovery and measurement.

We will avoid premature marketplace development, fleet ownership and complex automation.

The first version must make pickup commitments visible, failures actionable and outcomes measurable.

The next development phase is UI/UX Design, where we will translate the defined requirements into the actual screens, interactions and operational workflows required to build Dispatch.
