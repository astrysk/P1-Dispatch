DISPATCH
PRODUCT DESIGN & USER EXPERIENCE
October 2026

Phase Objective

Translate Dispatch's product strategy and requirements into a complete, testable user experience that can guide development.

Dispatch is designed to help small and medium-sized manufacturers manage scheduled pickups, confirm transporter commitments, identify potential failures, coordinate recovery, and measure outcomes.

The interface must help an operator answer five questions quickly:

1. What pickups are scheduled?
2. Which pickups are confirmed?
3. Which pickups require attention?
4. What action should be taken when a pickup fails?
5. What was the final outcome?

The product should prioritize operational clarity, fast task completion, reliable records, and minimal coordination overhead.

Design Principles

1. Action Before Analytics: Prioritize pickups that require attention over decorative charts and aggregate statistics.
2. Commitment Before Assignment: Clearly distinguish a transporter being assigned from a transporter confirming the pickup.
3. Exceptions Must Be Visible: Failed confirmations, overdue pickups, cancellations, and unresolved recovery attempts must not disappear inside ordinary status lists.
4. Every Important Action Must Be Traceable: Record relevant status changes, confirmation events, and recovery attempts.
5. Minimize Operator Effort: Reduce unnecessary navigation, repeated data entry, and ambiguous decisions.
6. Do Not Invent Certainty: Distinguish confirmed information from estimates, unverified reports, and missing information.
7. Design for Real Operations: Assume operators may manage several pickups simultaneously and work under time pressure.
8. Validate Before Expanding: Design only the workflows needed to test the core pickup-reliability proposition.

3.1 INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

3.1.1 Primary Product Structure

Dispatch will use an operations-first information architecture.

The primary interface should revolve around the pickup lifecycle rather than a large collection of disconnected modules.

Main Navigation

1. Overview
2. Pickups
3. Recovery
4. Transporters
5. Reports
6. Settings

1. Overview

Purpose: Provide an immediate view of the current operational situation.

Contains:

- Today's scheduled pickups.
- Pickups awaiting confirmation.
- Upcoming pickups.
- At-risk pickups.
- Overdue pickups.
- Active recovery cases.
- Recently completed pickups.
- Unresolved exceptions.

Primary action: Create Pickup.

The Overview page should help the operator identify the next action without requiring them to inspect every record individually.

2. Pickups

Purpose: Create, organize, monitor, and manage pickup records.

Contains:

- All pickups.
- Today's pickups.
- Upcoming pickups.
- Awaiting confirmation.
- Confirmed pickups.
- At-risk pickups.
- Delayed pickups.
- Completed pickups.
- Cancelled pickups.
- Unresolved pickups.

Each pickup should have a dedicated detail page containing its shipment information, transporter assignment, confirmation status, event history, exceptions, and recovery information.

Primary action: Create Pickup.

3. Recovery

Purpose: Manage pickups that cannot proceed as originally planned.

Contains:

- Recovery required.
- Recovery in progress.
- Alternative transporter attempts.
- Alternative commitments.
- Successfully recovered pickups.
- Unresolved recovery cases.

Each recovery case must be linked to its original pickup.

The interface should make the next required action visible, such as contacting an alternative transporter or confirming a revised pickup time.

Primary action: Start Recovery.

4. Transporters

Purpose: Maintain basic transporter information and review operational history.

Contains:

- Transporter directory.
- Contact information.
- Service areas.
- Available operational details.
- Associated pickup history.
- Recorded delays and exceptions.
- Historical completion and reliability metrics.

Transporter performance must be based on recorded evidence rather than unsupported ratings.

Primary action: Add Transporter.

5. Reports

Purpose: Understand pickup performance and operational efficiency.

Contains:

- Pickup volume.
- Confirmation rate.
- On-time pickup rate.
- Missed pickup rate.
- Recovery rate.
- Average pickup delay.
- Exception breakdown.
- Transporter performance summaries.

Reports should support a defined date range and clearly display the number of records included in each calculation.

Primary action: Review Performance.

6. Settings

Purpose: Configure the operational environment.

Contains:

- Organization details.
- User accounts.
- Roles and permissions.
- Pickup status definitions.
- Pickup timing rules.
- Notification preferences.
- Basic operational settings.

Advanced configuration should be excluded until a demonstrated need exists.

3.1.2 Core Information Hierarchy

The product should organize information in the following order:

Level 1: Operational Priority

What needs attention now?

Level 2: Pickup Status

What is happening with each scheduled pickup?

Level 3: Pickup Details

Who is responsible, where is the pickup happening, and when is it expected?

Level 4: Actions

What can the operator do next?

Level 5: History and Evidence

What happened previously, and what information supports the current status?

This hierarchy should remain consistent across the dashboard, pickup list, pickup detail page, and recovery workflow.

3.1.3 Pickup Record Structure

Every pickup should contain the following information.

A. Identification

- Unique pickup reference.
- Customer or business name.
- Shipment description.
- Created date and time.
- Record owner.

B. Pickup Details

- Pickup location.
- Destination.
- Scheduled pickup date.
- Scheduled pickup time.
- Expected shipment readiness.
- Special instructions.

C. Transporter Details

- Assigned transporter.
- Primary contact.
- Contact information.
- Assignment timestamp.

D. Commitment Details

- Confirmation status.
- Confirmation timestamp.
- Confirmed pickup time.
- Revised commitment, if applicable.
- Reason for rescheduling.

E. Execution Details

- Current pickup status.
- Actual arrival time, if recorded.
- Actual pickup completion time, if recorded.
- Reported delay.
- Exception reason.

F. Recovery Details

- Recovery status.
- Recovery initiation time.
- Alternative transporters contacted.
- Responses received.
- Alternative commitment.
- Final recovery outcome.

G. Activity History

- Event description.
- Timestamp.
- Actor or source.
- Previous status, where relevant.
- New status, where relevant.
- Supporting notes.

The system must distinguish between an event that occurred and an event that someone reported.

For example, a transporter reporting that a vehicle has arrived is different from a pickup being verified as completed.

3.1.4 Navigation Rules

- Every pickup should be accessible from the main pickup list.
- Clicking a pickup should open its detail view.
- Recovery cases should link directly to the associated pickup.
- Transporter profiles should link to their pickup history.
- Reports should allow users to inspect the underlying records where practical.
- Important actions should be available from both the pickup detail page and relevant operational lists.
- Navigation should preserve relevant filters when users return to a list.
- Destructive actions should require appropriate confirmation.
- Permissions should determine which actions are available to each user.

Information Architecture Decision

I recommend keeping the MVP navigation limited to six primary sections.

The product should not introduce separate modules for fleet management, route planning, invoicing, warehouse management, or freight procurement unless customer validation establishes a clear need.

3.2 USER JOURNEY MAPS

The initial design will focus on three primary journeys.

1. Create and confirm a scheduled pickup.
2. Identify and recover a failed pickup.
3. Review pickup performance.

These journeys cover the core operational value proposition.

3.2.1 Journey A: Create and Confirm a Pickup

Stage 1: Identify the Shipment

User Goal:

Schedule transportation for an outbound shipment.

User Actions:

- Open Dispatch.
- Select Create Pickup.
- Enter shipment information.
- Enter pickup and destination details.
- Select the scheduled time.

Potential Pain Points:

- Missing shipment information.
- Incorrect addresses.
- Unclear pickup timing.
- Repeated manual data entry.

Design Opportunities:

- Use clearly labeled fields.
- Validate required information.
- Allow the operator to review the pickup before saving.
- Provide useful defaults without silently inventing information.

System Response:

Create the pickup record and display its unique reference.

Stage 2: Assign a Transporter

User Goal:

Identify the transporter responsible for the pickup.

User Actions:

- Select an existing transporter.
- Review contact details.
- Assign the transporter.
- Initiate the confirmation process.

Potential Pain Points:

- Incorrect contact details.
- Unclear responsibility.
- Assuming that assignment means confirmation.

Design Opportunities:

- Display transporter identity and contact information.
- Separate assignment from confirmation.
- Make the confirmation status immediately visible.

System Response:

Set the pickup to Awaiting Confirmation.

Stage 3: Obtain Confirmation

User Goal:

Establish whether the transporter has accepted the pickup commitment.

User Actions:

- Contact the transporter.
- Record the response.
- Enter the confirmed time if it differs from the original request.

Potential Pain Points:

- No response.
- Conflicting pickup times.
- Informal verbal commitments.
- Unrecorded changes.

Design Opportunities:

- Provide explicit confirmation controls.
- Record the confirmation timestamp and source.
- Display unanswered requests prominently.

System Response:

Update the pickup to Confirmed only when the commitment has been explicitly acknowledged and recorded.

Stage 4: Monitor the Pickup

User Goal:

Know whether the pickup remains on schedule.

User Actions:

- Review the operational dashboard.
- Inspect the scheduled pickup.
- Record relevant updates.

Potential Pain Points:

- Transporter becomes unavailable.
- Pickup time passes without an update.
- The operator must repeatedly contact the transporter.

Design Opportunities:

- Highlight upcoming and overdue pickups.
- Surface missing confirmations.
- Show the next required action.

System Response:

Display the current status and flag pickups requiring attention according to defined operational rules.

Journey Completion

The journey is complete when the pickup reaches a recorded final outcome.

The operator should be able to determine whether the shipment was collected on time, collected late, recovered after a failure, cancelled, or left unresolved.

3.2.2 Journey B: Recover a Failed Pickup

Stage 1: Detect the Problem

User Goal:

Determine whether a scheduled pickup has failed or is at risk.

Trigger Examples:

- The transporter reports a cancellation.
- The transporter becomes unreachable.
- The scheduled time passes without arrival confirmation.
- The transporter reports a vehicle problem.

System Response:

Flag the pickup for attention.

The system should not automatically classify every overdue pickup as a confirmed failure. It should distinguish between an overdue record and a verified missed pickup.

Stage 2: Assess the Situation

User Goal:

Understand the cause and urgency of the problem.

User Actions:

- Open the pickup.
- Review the original commitment.
- Inspect recent activity.
- Record the reported exception.
- Determine whether the shipment remains ready for collection.

Potential Pain Points:

- Conflicting information.
- Unknown transporter availability.
- Unclear responsibility.
- Incomplete shipment details.

Design Opportunities:

- Display the original commitment and latest updates together.
- Record the reported reason.
- Make missing information visible.
- Avoid forcing the operator through unnecessary screens.

Stage 3: Initiate Recovery

User Goal:

Find a workable alternative.

User Actions:

- Start a recovery case.
- Identify a potential alternative transporter.
- Record contact attempts.
- Record responses.
- Confirm the alternative commitment.

Potential Pain Points:

- No alternative transporter is available.
- Contact attempts receive no response.
- Replacement costs are unclear.
- The new pickup time is uncertain.

Design Opportunities:

- Keep recovery actions in one interface.
- Record each alternative attempt.
- Distinguish proposed alternatives from accepted commitments.
- Allow the operator to record an unsuccessful recovery attempt.

System Response:

Set the recovery status to In Progress and preserve the original pickup history.

Stage 4: Confirm the Outcome

User Goal:

Establish whether the shipment was successfully collected.

User Actions:

- Record the revised pickup commitment.
- Monitor the alternative pickup.
- Record the actual outcome.
- Close the recovery case when appropriate.

Potential Pain Points:

- The alternative transporter also fails.
- The revised time changes again.
- The final outcome is not reported.

Design Opportunities:

- Keep unresolved cases visible.
- Record successive attempts.
- Distinguish successful recovery from a new commitment that has not yet been fulfilled.

System Response:

Update the pickup and recovery records with the final outcome.

Journey Completion

The journey is complete when the pickup has a documented outcome or is explicitly marked unresolved.

A recovery case must not be treated as successful simply because an alternative transporter was contacted.

3.2.3 Journey C: Review Pickup Performance

Stage 1: Open Reports

User Goal:

Understand how reliably pickups are being completed.

User Actions:

- Open Reports.
- Select a date range.
- Review the summary metrics.

Design Opportunities:

- Use clear metric definitions.
- Display the number of records included.
- Separate operational performance from incomplete data.

Stage 2: Investigate Exceptions

User Goal:

Understand why pickups are failing or being delayed.

User Actions:

- Review exception categories.
- Compare transporter histories.
- Inspect individual pickup records.

Design Opportunities:

- Link aggregate metrics to relevant pickup records.
- Distinguish transporter-related issues from manufacturer-related issues.
- Show the time period and sample size.

Stage 3: Identify Improvements

User Goal:

Determine what should change operationally.

Potential Actions:

- Improve confirmation procedures.
- Contact transporters earlier.
- Identify recurring failure patterns.
- Improve backup arrangements.
- Adjust scheduling practices.

Journey Completion

The user should be able to identify operational patterns and make better-informed decisions.

Dispatch should not imply that a transporter is unreliable or that performance has improved unless the underlying records support that conclusion.

3.3 USER FLOWS

3.3.1 Flow A: Create a Pickup

Start

→ Open Overview or Pickups

→ Select Create Pickup

→ Enter pickup details

→ Validate required fields

→ Assign transporter

→ Review pickup information

→ Save pickup

→ System creates unique pickup reference

→ Status becomes Awaiting Confirmation

→ Pickup appears in the operational dashboard

End

Alternative Paths

If required information is missing:

→ Display field-level validation

→ Preserve valid entered information

→ Allow the operator to correct the record

If the transporter is not available in the directory:

→ Allow the operator to save the pickup without a confirmed transporter assignment, if the workflow permits

→ Mark the record as requiring assignment

→ Keep the pickup visible for follow-up

If the save operation fails:

→ Display an error

→ Preserve entered information where possible

→ Allow the operator to retry

→ Avoid creating duplicate pickup records

3.3.2 Flow B: Confirm a Pickup

Start

→ Open pickup record

→ Review assigned transporter

→ Initiate confirmation

→ Record transporter response

Decision: Did the transporter accept the commitment?

If Yes:

→ Record confirmation timestamp

→ Record confirmed pickup time

→ Set status to Confirmed

→ Display confirmation in the pickup history

If No:

→ Record decline

→ Keep the pickup unconfirmed

→ Allow reassignment or another operational action

If No Response:

→ Keep the pickup Awaiting Confirmation

→ Display the pending action

→ Allow the operator to follow up or reassign the transporter

End

A transporter assignment must not automatically change the status to Confirmed.

3.3.3 Flow C: Monitor an Upcoming Pickup

Start

→ Open Overview

→ Review upcoming pickups

→ Select pickup

→ Inspect scheduled time and confirmation status

Decision: Is the pickup confirmed?

If No:

→ Highlight the missing confirmation

→ Allow the operator to contact or reassign the transporter

If Yes:

→ Continue monitoring

Decision: Has a qualifying risk or exception been recorded?

If No:

→ Keep the pickup in the upcoming or confirmed state

If Yes:

→ Flag the pickup

→ Display the relevant reason

→ Allow the operator to assess the situation

Decision: Has the scheduled pickup time passed without an outcome?

If Yes:

→ Flag the pickup as overdue

→ Request an update or allow an exception to be recorded

→ Do not automatically declare the pickup a confirmed failure

End

3.3.4 Flow D: Recover a Failed Pickup

Start

→ Open at-risk or overdue pickup

→ Review commitment and activity history

→ Record the exception

→ Select Start Recovery

→ Identify an alternative transporter

→ Record contact attempt

Decision: Is the alternative available?

If No:

→ Record the unsuccessful attempt

→ Try another alternative if available

→ If no alternative exists, retain the case as unresolved

If Yes:

→ Record the alternative commitment

→ Set recovery status to In Progress

→ Monitor the revised pickup

Decision: Was the pickup completed?

If Yes:

→ Record the actual outcome

→ Mark the pickup Completed, with recovery outcome recorded

→ Close the recovery case

If No:

→ Record the new exception

→ Continue recovery or mark unresolved

End

3.3.5 Flow E: Review Performance

Start

→ Open Reports

→ Select date range

→ Review pickup metrics

→ Select metric or exception category

→ Inspect relevant pickup records

→ Review transporter history

→ Identify recurring operational issues

End

If the selected period contains insufficient data:

→ Display the available record count

→ Explain that the metric may be inconclusive

→ Avoid presenting the result as reliable evidence of improvement

3.5 UI/UX DESIGN

3.5.1 Product Experience Direction

Dispatch should feel like a focused operational tool rather than a consumer delivery application.

The design should emphasize:

- Clarity.
- Accountability.
- Speed.
- Consistency.
- Operational visibility.
- Trustworthy information.

The interface should avoid excessive animations, decorative dashboards, unnecessary illustrations, and complicated navigation.

3.5.2 Visual Hierarchy

The visual hierarchy should follow this order:

1. Urgent operational issues.
2. Pickup status and scheduled time.
3. Primary actions.
4. Pickup and transporter details.
5. Activity history.
6. Supporting metrics.

A pickup requiring immediate attention should be visually distinguishable from an ordinary confirmed pickup.

The interface should communicate urgency through a combination of labels, positioning, icons, and accessible color use.

3.5.3 Status Design

Status labels must use consistent language across the dashboard, pickup list, detail page, and reports.

Requested:

The pickup request has been created.

Awaiting Confirmation:

A transporter has not yet explicitly confirmed the commitment.

Confirmed:

The transporter has accepted the recorded pickup commitment.

At Risk:

There is a recorded reason to believe the pickup may not proceed as planned.

Recovery in Progress:

An operator is coordinating an alternative or otherwise attempting to resolve a failure.

Completed:

The pickup has been recorded as completed.

Delayed:

The pickup has been delayed, but its final completion status may still need to be recorded.

Cancelled:

The pickup has been cancelled.

Unresolved:

The pickup outcome or recovery remains unresolved.

The data model must distinguish current operational status from historical events and final outcomes. A completed pickup may have a recovery history without losing its final Completed status.

3.5.4 Primary Actions

Each screen should have one visually dominant primary action.

Examples:

- Overview: Create Pickup.
- Pickup Detail: The most relevant next action for the current status.
- Recovery Workspace: Add Recovery Attempt or Record Outcome.
- Reports: Review Records or Export, if export is implemented.

Secondary actions should be visually subordinate.

Destructive actions should never be presented as the default primary action.

3.5.5 Forms and Data Entry

Forms should:

- Group related information.
- Use descriptive field labels.
- Mark required fields.
- Provide appropriate field-level validation.
- Prevent invalid date and time combinations.
- Preserve valid information when an error occurs.
- Clearly explain why an action cannot proceed.
- Avoid unnecessary mandatory fields.
- Support efficient keyboard navigation.

3.5.6 Feedback and Confirmation

The system should provide clear feedback when an action succeeds or fails.

Examples:

Successful:

Pickup created. Reference: DSP-000123.

Pending:

Pickup saved. Transporter confirmation is still pending.

Error:

Pickup could not be saved. Your entered information has been preserved where possible.

Confirmation:

Are you sure you want to cancel this pickup? The action will be recorded in the pickup history.

These are example messages. Actual reference generation and supported operations will depend on implementation.

3.5.7 Responsive Behavior

Desktop should be the primary design target for the initial operational dashboard, subject to validation of the intended users' working environment.

The interface should also accommodate narrower screens for operators who need to check a pickup or record an update away from their desks.

Desktop:

- Full navigation.
- Multi-column layouts.
- Detailed pickup tables.
- Side-by-side information where helpful.

Tablet:

- Reduced column density.
- Collapsible navigation.
- Prioritized pickup details.

Mobile:

- Compact navigation.
- Stacked information.
- Prominent status and next action.
- Simplified forms.
- Easy access to contact information.
- Avoidance of wide, difficult-to-use tables.

Responsive design should preserve the critical workflow rather than simply shrink the desktop interface.

3.5.8 Accessibility

The interface should follow established accessibility practices.

Requirements include:

- Readable text sizes.
- Sufficient text and interface contrast.
- Keyboard-accessible navigation.
- Visible focus indicators.
- Meaningful labels for form controls.
- Clear error descriptions.
- Status indicators that do not depend on color alone.
- Accessible names for interactive controls.
- Appropriate support for screen readers.

Accessibility should be addressed during design and implementation, not postponed until the product is complete.

- Identify a pickup awaiting confirmation.
- Determine which pickups require attention.
- Record an exception.
- Start and manage recovery.
- Find the final pickup outcome.
- Understand the reported performance metrics.

Record:

- Task completion.
- Time required.
- Errors.
- Points of confusion.
- Unnecessary actions.
- Misunderstood status labels.
- User feedback.

Usability thresholds should be defined before formal testing. Any initial thresholds are provisional until tested with representative users.

3.6.6 Prototype Completion Criteria

3.7 DESIGN SYSTEM

Establish a consistent set of interface rules and reusable components for the Dispatch MVP.

The initial system should be lightweight. It should provide enough consistency to support development without creating unnecessary design overhead.

3.7.2 Color System

Primary Surface:

White or near-white.

Secondary Surface:

Light neutral grey.

Primary Text:

Dark charcoal or near-black.

Secondary Text:

Medium neutral grey.

Primary Action:

A single consistent brand accent.

Success:

A clearly distinguishable success color.

Warning:

A clearly distinguishable warning color.

Critical:

A clearly distinguishable error or urgent-state color.

Borders:

Light neutral grey.

- Numeric metric.
- Helper and error text.

Use a consistent type scale and line-height system.

3.8 PRODUCT STATES & EDGE CASES

Dispatch operates in an environment where information can be delayed, incomplete, contradictory, or incorrect.

The interface must handle these situations explicitly.

3.8.1 Loading States

Scenario:

The system is retrieving pickup records or reports.

Expected Behavior:

- Display an appropriate loading indicator.
- Preserve the page structure where possible.
- Prevent duplicate submissions while a request is processing.
- Provide a retry option when loading fails.
- Avoid showing old data as current without indicating that it may be stale.

3.8.2 Empty States

Scenario:

No pickups have been created.

Expected Behavior:

- Explain that no pickups are available.
- Provide a Create Pickup action when the user has permission.
- Avoid displaying misleading zero-performance metrics.

Scenario:

No pickups match the selected filters.

Expected Behavior:

- Explain that no matching records were found.
- Allow the user to clear filters or change the search.
- Preserve the user's ability to navigate elsewhere.

Scenario:

No recovery cases exist.

Expected Behavior:

- Explain that no active recovery cases are recorded.
- Avoid suggesting that no pickup failures have ever occurred unless historical data supports that conclusion.

3.8.3 Form Validation Errors

Scenario:

Required pickup information is missing.

Expected Behavior:

- Identify the missing fields.
- Explain what is required.
- Preserve valid entered information.
- Prevent invalid submission.

Scenario:

The scheduled time is invalid.

Expected Behavior:

- Explain the problem.
- Allow correction.
- Validate the relationship between the scheduled time and relevant operational rules.

Scenario:

The pickup location or destination is incomplete.

Expected Behavior:

- Identify the affected field.
- Request the missing information.
- Avoid silently substituting an address.

3.8.4 Transporter Does Not Respond

Scenario:

A transporter has been assigned but has not confirmed the pickup.

Expected Behavior:

- Keep the pickup in Awaiting Confirmation.
- Display the pending confirmation.
- Allow the operator to follow up or reassign the transporter.
- Record the follow-up action where supported.

The system must not assume that silence means acceptance.

3.8.5 Transporter Cancels After Confirmation

Scenario:

A transporter withdraws from a previously confirmed pickup.

Expected Behavior:

- Record the cancellation event.
- Preserve the original commitment.
- Flag the pickup for attention.
- Allow the operator to initiate recovery.
- Record alternative transporter attempts.
- Maintain the final outcome separately from the original commitment.

The system must not erase the original confirmation when the commitment changes.

3.8.6 Pickup Is Overdue but the Outcome Is Unknown

Scenario:

The scheduled pickup time has passed, but no arrival or completion information is available.

Expected Behavior:

- Mark the pickup as requiring attention.
- Display the scheduled time.
- Allow the operator to contact the transporter or record an exception.
- Distinguish an overdue record from a verified missed pickup.

An overdue pickup should not automatically be classified as a confirmed failure.

3.8.7 Alternative Transporter Is Unavailable

Scenario:

The operator contacts an alternative transporter, but the transporter declines or cannot provide a vehicle.

Expected Behavior:

- Record the attempt.
- Preserve the response.
- Allow another recovery attempt.
- Keep the case unresolved if no workable alternative is available.

The system must not treat contacting an alternative transporter as successful recovery.

3.8.8 Recovery Also Fails

Scenario:

An alternative transporter accepts the revised commitment but fails to complete the pickup.

Expected Behavior:

- Record the new failure.
- Preserve the earlier recovery history.
- Allow another recovery attempt.
- Keep the final outcome accurate.
- Prevent the original failure from disappearing from the record.

3.8.9 Manufacturer Is Not Ready

Scenario:

The transporter arrives, but the shipment is not ready for collection.

Expected Behavior:

- Record the relevant exception.
- Identify the reported cause.
- Record the revised commitment where available.
- Distinguish the manufacturer-related delay from a transporter-related failure.

This distinction is essential for fair performance reporting.

3.8.10 Pickup Details Change

Scenario:

The pickup time, destination, or shipment details change after confirmation.

Expected Behavior:

- Record the change.
- Preserve the previous value in the history.
- Update the current record.
- Reconfirm the commitment when the change materially affects transporter acceptance.
- Make the revised commitment visible.

3.8.11 Duplicate Pickup Submission

Scenario:

An operator submits a form twice because the system responds slowly.

Expected Behavior:

- Prevent duplicate creation where possible.
- Provide feedback while the request is processing.
- Use appropriate server-side safeguards for duplicate submissions.
- Display the existing pickup record if the system can reliably identify the repeated request.

3.8.12 Network or Server Failure

Scenario:

The operator attempts to save a pickup or record a status change while the service is unavailable.

Expected Behavior:

- Display a clear error.
- Avoid falsely confirming that the action succeeded.
- Preserve entered information where possible.
- Allow the operator to retry.
- Ensure that retries do not create duplicate events or records.

3.8.13 Conflicting Information

Scenario:

The manufacturer reports that the pickup did not happen, while the transporter reports that the vehicle arrived.

Expected Behavior:

- Preserve both reports and their sources.
- Avoid silently selecting one account as definitive.
- Allow the operator to investigate and record the resolution.
- Distinguish reported information from verified outcomes.

3.8.14 Permission Restrictions

Scenario:

A user attempts to perform an action they are not authorized to execute.

Expected Behavior:

- Prevent the unauthorized action.
- Explain that the user lacks permission.
- Avoid exposing restricted information.
- Provide an appropriate route to an authorized operator when necessary.

Permissions must be enforced by the backend, not only hidden in the interface.

3.8.15 Cancellation and Completion Conflicts

Scenario:

A user attempts to cancel a pickup that has already been completed.

Expected Behavior:

- Prevent an invalid transition.
- Explain the current status.
- Provide a controlled correction process if the recorded outcome is wrong.
- Preserve the audit history.

3.8.16 Missing Historical Data

Scenario:

A pickup record does not contain enough information to calculate a performance metric.

Expected Behavior:

- Exclude the record from calculations where necessary.
- Display the eligible record count.
- Explain the limitation.
- Avoid interpreting missing information as a successful pickup.

3.8.17 Edge-Case Completion Criteria

The design is complete when:

- Critical operational states are documented.
- Validation behavior is defined.
- Failure recovery is represented.
- Permissions are accounted for.
- Important status transitions are controlled.
- Historical records remain traceable.
- Missing and conflicting information is handled explicitly.
- The interface avoids presenting unverified information as fact.

PHASE 3: FINAL DESIGN DECISIONS

Decision 1: Build Around the Pickup Lifecycle

The primary product experience will revolve around creating, confirming, monitoring, recovering, and reviewing pickups.

Decision 2: Make Operational Exceptions the Priority

The dashboard will prioritize pickups requiring action rather than emphasizing decorative analytics.

Decision 3: Preserve the Difference Between Commitment and Outcome

A confirmed pickup is not necessarily a completed pickup. An overdue pickup is not necessarily a verified failure. A recovery attempt is not necessarily a successful recovery.

The data model and interface must preserve these distinctions.

Decision 4: Keep Recovery Linked to the Original Pickup

Recovery attempts must retain the original commitment and failure history. This will allow Dispatch to measure both the initial failure and the final outcome.

Decision 5: Use a Lightweight Design System

The first version will use a small set of reusable components and consistent visual rules. A more extensive design system can be introduced when justified.

Decision 6: Validate the Experience Before Full Development

The core workflows should be demonstrated through an interactive prototype and reviewed against realistic operational scenarios before full implementation.

Decision 7: Treat Operational Evidence as a Product Requirement

The design must support accurate status histories, exception records, confirmation timestamps, and final outcomes. These records will form the basis for evaluating whether Dispatch creates measurable value.
